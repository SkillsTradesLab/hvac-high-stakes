House rules

Play the hand.
Know your trade.

Students

  1. Join the teacher's room with your name or initials. Eight seats; $1,500 virtual starting chips each.
  2. Put up $100 before each question. This is the fixed default. If the teacher chooses optional wagering, you can choose more, in $50 steps.
  3. When the teacher deals, a 3-second cue precedes the shared 30-second answer window. Choose one answer. Your first accepted answer locks.
  4. A correct answer returns your wager and pays matching profit. Applied questions add $50; Advanced questions add $100. Wrong answers and timeouts lose the wager.
  5. Less than $100 means you are out. Keep watching: another student may voluntarily rescue you between hands.
  6. A rescuer gives half of current net winnings above their initial $1,500, rounded down. It must give you enough to play and leave the donor enough to play. One rescue received per student by default.
  7. The selected hand limit ends the game. Fewer than two active players after the rescue window also ends play. Highest ending balance wins; correct answers break a tie.

If you do not lock a wager, the deal automatically commits $100 when you can afford it. Disconnection does not cancel a wager. Your answer must reach the teacher before the clock ends. These are virtual learning chips.

Teacher overview

Open the teacher table, select chapters and difficulty, choose the hand limit, then share the student link. Wait for all eight Connected seats before starting. Deal each question and allow explanation and rescue time before the next hand.

You can see balances, connections, wagers, answer progress, accuracy, eliminations, and rescue activity. Export the CSV for individual learning review. Keep the host tab open and your computer awake.

The 269-question pool contains 45 refrigeration, 64 circuitry, and 160 motor questions. It includes 70 calculation forms using varied values. Questions and choices shuffle; prior-question history in your browser favors fresh questions for replay.

Test your classroom connection

The live game uses external PeerJS signaling and direct browser connections. Run a test hand on all eight devices using the actual school network before class. Restricted networks may prevent connections. The same-computer tab test is a rehearsal mode and does not connect separate devices.
